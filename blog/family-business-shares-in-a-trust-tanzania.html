Putting Family Business Shares in a Trust in Tanzania

Many family businesses do not die with the founder. They die a few years later, when shares are split among heirs who cannot agree.

Abdulrahman SuddyBusiness & Financial Advisory · Last updated: 19 November 2026 · 8 min read
Quick answerPlacing family business shares in a trust means transferring them to trustees (or a registered trust) who hold and vote them for the family under a trust deed. The business stays as one block, heirs share the economic benefit, and a clear process decides who sits on the board. The transfer must be recorded in the company's share register and BRELA filings, beneficial ownership must be disclosed, and stamp duty and tax consequences must be checked.

The problem trusts solve

When a founder dies holding shares personally, those shares pass through the estate and are often divided among many heirs. Each heir gains a vote, and decisions slow down or stop. The Rwakatare estate case also shows how unclear lines between company assets and the founder's estate can end up in court. See the Rwakatare case.

How the structure works

  1. The founder transfers shares, often of a holding company, to the trust.
  2. Trustees become the registered shareholders.
  3. The deed tells trustees how to vote, how to appoint directors and how to share dividends.
  4. Directors run the business; trustees oversee ownership.
  5. Family members benefit as beneficiaries, without each holding votes.

Practical steps

  • Check the company's articles for restrictions on transfers.
  • Value the shares and calculate stamp duty on the transfer.
  • Take tax advice on any gain triggered by the transfer.
  • Update the share register and file the change with BRELA.
  • File beneficial ownership information, identifying the real people behind the trust.
  • Sign a shareholders' agreement if there are other shareholders.

Governance tips

  • Separate the roles of trustee and director where possible.
  • Set dividend policy in writing so expectations are clear.
  • Create a family council or annual family meeting for communication.

Frequently asked questions

Can a trust own shares in a Tanzanian company?

Yes. Trustees can be registered as shareholders, and a registered trust can hold shares in its own name. The transfer must be recorded in the share register and BRELA filings.

Do I lose control of my company if I put shares in a trust?

It depends on the deed. Founders often remain a trustee or director during their lifetime, with succession rules taking over later.

Is stamp duty payable when transferring shares to a trust?

Stamp duty generally applies to share transfers in Tanzania, including transfers without payment. Confirm the position with a tax adviser before transferring.
