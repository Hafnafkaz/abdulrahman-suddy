Letter of Wishes: The Document That Guides Your Trustees

The trust deed tells trustees what they must do. The letter of wishes tells them what you would want. Both matter.

Abdulrahman SuddyBusiness & Financial Advisory · Last updated: 19 November 2026 · 6 min read
Quick answerA letter of wishes is a non-binding document from the settlor to the trustees explaining how they would like the trustees' discretion to be used: priorities, values, the needs of particular beneficiaries and how to handle the family business. It sits alongside the legally binding trust deed, can be updated easily without amending the deed, and is one of the best tools to prevent misunderstandings.

Why it matters

Good trust deeds give trustees discretion, because no one can foresee every situation. A letter of wishes tells them how you would think about those situations.

What to include

  • The purpose of the trust in your own words.
  • Your priorities: education, health, housing, business continuity.
  • Specific needs of individual beneficiaries.
  • Your views on when and how capital should be released.
  • Guidance on the family business and who should be involved.
  • Values you hope the family keeps.

What not to do

  • Do not contradict the trust deed. The deed wins.
  • Do not use it for binding instructions; put those in the deed.
  • Do not forget to sign, date and update it.

Keep it current

Review the letter after births, deaths, marriages, divorces and big business changes. Give a copy to your trustees and keep one with the trust deed.

Frequently asked questions

Is a letter of wishes legally binding in Tanzania?

No. It is guidance to trustees. Binding terms belong in the trust deed.

Can I change my letter of wishes?

Yes, at any time, without amending the trust deed. Sign and date each new version.

Who should hold the letter of wishes?

Give copies to your trustees and keep one with your trust deed and will.
