How to Set Up a Trust for Your Children's Education in Tanzania

Most parents' biggest fear is not death. It is what happens to their children's schooling afterwards. An education trust answers that fear in writing.

Abdulrahman SuddyBusiness & Financial Advisory · Last updated: 19 November 2026 · 7 min read
Quick answerAn education trust is a family trust whose main purpose is paying for children's schooling and upkeep. You transfer assets or income-producing property to trustees, and the trust deed tells them to pay school fees, books, transport and living costs directly or in instalments until set ages or milestones. It protects money from being spent early, keeps children from receiving large sums too young, and gives guardians a reliable source of funds.

Why parents choose an education trust

  • Fees keep flowing. Trustees can pay schools directly, term by term.
  • No lump sums to teenagers. Money is released for purposes, not handed over all at once.
  • Support for guardians. Whoever raises the children has a predictable source of funds.
  • Less family pressure. Clear rules make it harder for relatives to redirect money.

How to structure it

  1. Define the purpose. Schooling, university or vocational training, upkeep, medical care.
  2. Name the beneficiaries. Specific children, or "my children and grandchildren" as a class.
  3. Set the payout rules. Direct payment of fees, monthly upkeep, a final distribution at a chosen age.
  4. Choose the assets. Rental property, shares, investments or cash that produce income.
  5. Pick trustees who can say no. They must protect the purpose, even under pressure.
  6. Link it to your will and guardianship choice. The guardian and the trustees should work together, but they do not have to be the same person.

Living trust or through your will?

A trust in your will is simpler and costs less to start, but it waits for the estate process. A living trust starts now and keeps assets outside the estate. Read living vs testamentary trusts.

Common pitfalls

  • Naming a guardian but leaving no money structure behind.
  • Making the only trustee the guardian, with no independent check.
  • Forgetting to update the trust when new children are born.

Frequently asked questions

Can I set up a trust only for school fees?

Yes. The trust deed can limit payments to education and related costs, paid directly to institutions if you prefer.

At what age should children receive trust money?

You decide in the deed. Many parents release support for education first and any remaining capital later, at an age they consider mature.

Should the guardian also be the trustee?

They can be, but it is safer to have at least one other trustee so there is a check on how money is used.
