Beneficial Ownership in Tanzania: What Every Company Owner Must File

If you own more than a quarter of a Tanzanian company, or control it, the registrar needs to know your name. This is the most common compliance gap in family businesses.

Abdulrahman SuddyBusiness & Financial Advisory · 19 November 2026 · 6 min read

Under the Companies Act, as amended, companies in Tanzania must give the Registrar of Companies information about their beneficial owners. The rules apply to private and public companies and to branches of foreign companies.

Who is a beneficial owner?

A beneficial owner is a real person who ultimately owns or controls the company. The reporting threshold is more than 25% of the shares, or more than 25% of the voting or decision-making power. That means you have to look through a holding company to the people behind it. A company is not a beneficial owner on its own.

What you must do

  • File the information with BRELA for every beneficial owner: identity details and the nature of the interest.
  • Report changes within 30 days of the company becoming aware of them.
  • Keep your records consistent. The share register, the BRELA filing and your bank's records should agree.

The deadline has moved before

The filing deadline was extended several times after the regulations were introduced in 2021. A BRELA notice in March 2025 required local companies to file by 15 April 2025, and commentators have said companies that missed that date are already non-compliant. Penalties for non-compliance exist, but published figures differ between sources. Check the current BRELA notice and the Companies (Beneficial Ownership) Regulations for today's position rather than relying on an article.

How holding structures change the picture

Adding a holding company or a trust does not remove the filing duty. It means the company must identify the natural persons at the top of the chain. If a trust holds shares, the trustees and the people who benefit or control may need to be identified. Ask your advocate how the rules apply to your structure.

A five-point check this week

  1. List everyone who holds more than 25% of shares or votes, including through other companies.
  2. Compare that list to what BRELA has on record.
  3. File or update anything that is missing or out of date.
  4. Check your share register against your latest shareholders' resolutions.
  5. Diarise a 30-day change reminder every time ownership moves.
