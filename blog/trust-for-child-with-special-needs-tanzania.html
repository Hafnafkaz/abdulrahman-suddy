Protecting a Child with Special Needs: Using a Trust in Tanzania

For parents of a child with a disability, the question is not "who inherits" but "who will look after them, and with what money, for the rest of their life".

Abdulrahman SuddyBusiness & Financial Advisory · Last updated: 19 November 2026 · 7 min read
Quick answerA care trust holds assets for a dependant with special needs and lets trustees pay for care, therapy, medical costs, housing and daily needs for life. Because the beneficiary does not hold the assets directly, money is protected from misuse and decisions are made by people you chose. The deed should set clear purposes, name a care adviser or guardian to work with trustees, and say what happens to remaining assets after the beneficiary's lifetime.

Why a trust fits this situation

  • The beneficiary may never be able to manage money safely.
  • Care needs last decades, not years.
  • Relatives may have good intentions but competing pressures.

Key design choices

  1. Purpose clause. Care, medical needs, therapy, housing, education and quality of life.
  2. Discretion. Give trustees flexibility to meet changing needs, with guidance in a letter of wishes.
  3. A care adviser. Someone close to the beneficiary who advises trustees, separate from those holding the money.
  4. Income-producing assets. Rental property or investments that fund care over time.
  5. Succession of trustees. Plan for trustees who will outlive you.
  6. Remainder. Who receives what is left after the beneficiary's lifetime.

Write a detailed letter of wishes

Describe routines, doctors, likes and dislikes, and what a good life looks like for your child. Trustees will rely on it more than anything else. See letter of wishes.

Start early

Set up the trust while you are well and can explain your wishes, and review it as your child's needs change.

Frequently asked questions

Can a trust provide for a disabled child for life?

Yes. A trust can last for the beneficiary's lifetime and pay for care, medical needs and daily living according to the deed.

Who should be trustee for a special needs trust?

At least two people: often a trusted relative and an independent person, with a separate care adviser who knows the beneficiary well.

What happens to the trust when the beneficiary dies?

The deed should name who receives any remaining assets, such as siblings or a charity.
