Family Trusts and Customary Law in Tanzania: Clan Land, Kinship and Modern Planning

Many Tanzanian families already think in trust-like ways: elders holding land for the clan, property kept for future generations. A formal trust can put that tradition on a legal footing.

Abdulrahman SuddyBusiness & Financial Advisory · Last updated: 19 November 2026 · 7 min read
Quick answerCustomary law can govern succession for some Tanzanian families and often treats land as family or clan property held for future generations. The Trustees' Incorporation Act expressly covers bodies bound together by custom or kinship, so families can formalise these arrangements as registered trusts. Doing so clarifies who holds the land, how decisions are made and who benefits, reducing disputes when elders pass away.

Tradition already looks like a trust

Elders often hold family land not for themselves, but for the family. That is the essence of a trust. The problem is that it is rarely written down, so every generation reopens the question.

The law recognises kinship trusts

The Trustees' Incorporation Act lets trustees of bodies bound together by custom, religion, kinship or nationality apply for incorporation. A family or clan can form a registered trust that holds land and property in its own name.

Why formalise?

  • Clear title. Land registered in the trust's name is easier to protect and harder to grab.
  • Continuity. When an elder dies, the trust continues; there is no need to rebuild authority.
  • Fairness. A deed can include daughters and widows explicitly, addressing inequalities critics identify in customary rules.
  • Evidence. Informal family minutes are weak evidence, as the High Court's 2024 Mberesero decision showed. A deed is strong.

How to begin

  1. Hold a family meeting to agree the purpose and the property.
  2. Formalise land titles where possible.
  3. Draft a deed with an advocate, reflecting agreed traditions.
  4. Choose trustees acceptable to all branches of the family.
  5. Register with RITA and transfer the property.

Frequently asked questions

Can a clan register a trust in Tanzania?

Yes. The Trustees' Incorporation Act covers bodies bound together by custom or kinship, so a family or clan can apply to incorporate its trustees.

Does a trust override customary inheritance?

A trust governs the property properly transferred into it according to its deed. Property outside the trust still follows the succession law that applies.

Are family meeting minutes enough to create a trust?

Usually not. In the 2024 Mberesero case, the High Court found family meeting minutes did not establish enforceable trustee duties. Use a proper deed.
