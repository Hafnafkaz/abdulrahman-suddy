Holding companies

Do You Need a Holding Company in Tanzania? What It Costs and When It Pays

Everyone says "put it in a holding company". In Tanzania the tax gain is smaller than people think, and on a sale it can run the wrong way. Here is how to decide with numbers.

Abdulrahman SuddyBusiness & Financial Advisory · 19 November 2026 · 8 min read

A holding company is a company whose main job is to own shares in other companies. You own the holding company. It owns your operating company, and maybe a property company, an investment portfolio, or a brand. The business that deals with customers and staff sits underneath, and the valuable assets can sit beside it.

This is how large groups are built around the world. Berkshire Hathaway is a holding company that owns GEICO, BNSF Railway and dozens of others. In 2015 Google created Alphabet as a parent holding company so that Google and its separate bets could be funded and run apart. The idea travels well. The tax and legal detail does not, so it is worth being precise about Tanzania.

What Tanzania does and does not give you

Some countries give holding companies a full exemption on dividends and gains from their subsidiaries. Tanzania does not have a general participation exemption or a special holding company regime. What it has is a reduced withholding tax rate on dividends in a specific case.

ItemRate
Company income tax30%
Dividend paid to a resident company holding 25% or more of the shares and voting power5% withholding tax
Dividend paid to an individual by an unlisted company10% withholding tax
Capital gain on Tanzanian shares: resident individual10%
Capital gain on Tanzanian shares: company (taxed as business income)30%
Stamp duty on a share transfer1% of the value of the shares

Source: PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries for Tanzania (reviewed September 2026) and the TRA stamp duty schedule. Check the current figures before acting, because the Finance Act can change them each July.

A worked example

Say your operating company earns TZS 300 million before tax. Company tax at 30% leaves TZS 210 million. You decide to pay out 60% as dividends (TZS 126 million) and, if there is a holding company, to reinvest 70% of what it receives.

  • Own the company directly: 10% dividend tax on TZS 126 million is TZS 12.6 million. You keep TZS 197.4 million of value.
  • Through a holding company: the dividend into the holdco is taxed at 5% (TZS 6.3 million). The 30% it pays out to you is taxed at 10% (TZS 3.6 million). You keep about TZS 200.1 million.
The difference is about TZS 2.7 million a year on TZS 300 million of profit. Moving the shares costs about TZS 5 million in stamp duty on a TZS 500 million valuation, before advocate and filing fees.

So a holding company in Tanzania is not a tax trick. The tax saving is real but modest. The reasons to use one are usually protection and flexibility, with tax as a small bonus.

When it does not pay

If you take most of the cash out personally, the holding company makes things worse. The money is taxed at 5% on the way into the holdco and again at 10% on the way out to you. In the example above, paying everything out personally costs about TZS 5.7 million more per year than owning directly.

The exit trap most founders miss

If you may sell your operating company within a few years, stop and model it first. A resident individual pays 10% capital gains tax on a gain from Tanzanian investments. A company selling the same shares includes the gain in income taxed at 30%. On a TZS 1 billion gain, that is TZS 100 million of tax as an individual against TZS 300 million through a holding company, and then another 10% if you pay the money out to yourself.

The Finance Act 2026 also confirms that when a transfer between associated persons is exempt, the next owner keeps the original cost base. There is no step-up in value. Ask your tax adviser whether any reorganisation relief applies when you first move the shares in, and what cost base you will carry.

Two rules to know before you start

  • The 25% test. The 5% rate needs the holding company to control at least 25% of the voting power and hold at least 25% of the shares.
  • Deemed dividends. From 1 July 2026 the Commissioner General can treat 15% of undistributed profits as paid out if dividends are not declared within 12 months after the year end. Listed companies, financial institutions, insurers and certain mining companies are excluded. Parking profit in a company forever is not free.

Where a holding company genuinely helps

  • Ring-fencing. Keep property, brand or cash in a separate company from the one that trades, and lease or license it to the trading company. If the trading company is sued or fails, the other assets are not automatically exposed.
  • Several ventures. A holdco gives each venture its own mix of owners without disturbing the others.
  • Succession. Passing on one company's shares is simpler than passing on many separate assets. It does not replace a will.
  • Reinvestment. If you reinvest most surplus profit, the lower dividend rate adds up over the years.

Compliance that comes with it

Every company must disclose its beneficial owners to BRELA. In practice that means anyone holding more than 25% of the shares or voting power, with changes reported within 30 days. A holding structure adds a layer, so the people behind it must still be identified. You also need a TIN, a share register that matches your filings, and annual returns.
