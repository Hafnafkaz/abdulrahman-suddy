12 Family Trust Mistakes Tanzanian Families Make (and How to Avoid Them)

Most failed trusts do not fail in court. They fail quietly, because of mistakes made on day one.

Abdulrahman SuddyBusiness & Financial Advisory · Last updated: 20 November 2026 · 7 min read
Quick answerThe most common family trust mistakes in Tanzania are: not transferring assets into the trust, vague trustee duties, a single trustee with no check, no successor trustees, ignoring the succession law that applies, mixing personal and trust property, no separate bank account, missed RITA filings, ignoring tax on transfers, no letter of wishes, keeping the plan secret, and never reviewing it.

The 12 mistakes

  1. The empty trust. A deed is signed but assets are never transferred.
  2. Vague duties. Duties not written clearly may not be enforceable, as the 2024 Mberesero case showed.
  3. One trustee, no check. Especially when that trustee is also a beneficiary.
  4. No successors. The trust stalls when a trustee dies.
  5. Ignoring succession law. Islamic and customary rules can affect property outside the trust.
  6. Mixing property. Personal, company and trust assets blur together.
  7. No separate account. Trust money passes through personal accounts.
  8. Missed filings. Annual returns and change notices are forgotten.
  9. Ignoring transfer tax. Stamp duty and capital gains come as a surprise.
  10. No letter of wishes. Trustees have discretion but no guidance.
  11. Secrecy. The family discovers the trust only at the funeral.
  12. Set and forget. No review after births, deaths, marriages or business changes.

A simple prevention plan

  • Use an experienced advocate and a separate tax adviser.
  • Transfer and register assets properly.
  • Appoint two or more trustees and successors.
  • Diarise compliance.
  • Talk to your family.
  • Review every year.

Frequently asked questions

What is the biggest mistake with family trusts?

Failing to transfer assets into the trust. A trust only protects what it actually owns.

Why do family trusts fail?

Usually because of vague deeds, poor trustee choices, mixing assets and missed compliance, rather than a legal defect.

How often should I review my trust?

At least once a year and after any major life or business event.
