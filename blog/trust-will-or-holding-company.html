Trusts & estate planning

Trust, Will or Holding Company: What Protects Your Family Best in Tanzania?

They sound like three answers to the same question. They are three different tools. Using the wrong one is how families end up in court.

Abdulrahman SuddyBusiness & Financial Advisory · 19 November 2026 · 8 min read

Most business owners hear three words from advisers: will, trust and holding company. They are often treated as alternatives. They are better understood as tools for different jobs. A will says who inherits. A holding company decides how a business is owned and where risk sits. A trust lets someone you choose hold and manage assets for people who cannot or should not hold them directly.

The will: the tool everyone needs

A valid will lets you name your heirs, an executor, and guardians for minor children. In Tanzania, estates are handled under the Probate and Administration of Estates Act, and which succession rules apply can depend on a person's faith and way of life. Courts have applied Islamic law to Muslims, customary law in some cases, and general law elsewhere, and a will is the way to state your wishes. Where those rules apply to your estate and how far a will can depart from them is a question for your advocate, not a guess.

The cost of having none is vivid abroad too. Prince died in 2016 with no will, and his estate, which was large and complex, went through a long court process before his heirs were identified. Even in a country with a polished legal system, no will means other people decide.

The holding company: ownership and risk

A holding company owns your shares and other assets. It helps you ring-fence risk, hold several ventures, and pass on one block of shares instead of many assets. It does not decide who inherits the holding company shares. That still needs a will. See our holding company guide for the tax and exit detail.

The trust: managing assets for others

In a trust, a settlor transfers assets to trustees who hold them for beneficiaries under a trust deed. It is useful when beneficiaries are minors, have special needs, or when you want assets managed over many years. A trust can be created during your lifetime or on death.

Here Tanzania differs from countries with well-developed trust statutes. The Trustees' Incorporation Act (Cap 318) provides for incorporating trustees who hold property for religious, educational, literary, scientific, social or charitable purposes, or for bodies bound by custom, religion, kinship or nationality. They apply to the Administrator-General (administered through RITA), and the incorporated body carries the name "Registered Trustees". That route suits schools, mosques, churches, foundations and community bodies. For a purely private family trust, published legal commentary describes reliance on the trust deed and general principles of equity rather than a dedicated private trust statute, so drafting quality matters a great deal and an advocate should prepare it.

Which tool for which job

Your situationStart with
You have no willA will, before anything else
One business, risk and assets mixed in your own nameSeparate the assets, and consider a holding company
Several ventures or partnersHolding company plus a shareholders' agreement
Minor children or a dependant who cannot manage moneyA trust deed drafted by an advocate, plus a will
Assets for a charity, school or place of worshipRegistered Trustees under Cap 318

Two traps in Tanzania

  • Land and nationality. Under the Land Act, a company whose shareholders are mostly non-citizens is treated as foreign and generally may hold only a derivative right, not a full right of occupancy. If heirs or shareholders are non-citizens, check before putting land in a company or leaving it by will.
  • Transferring shares costs money. Stamp duty on a share transfer is 1% of the value, whether or not it is paid for. Moving shares into a holding company or a trust is not free.

A sensible order

  1. Write or update your will.
  2. Get your company records in order: share register, shareholders' agreement, beneficial ownership filing.
  3. Decide whether separating assets in a holding company gives you real protection.
  4. Add a trust only if you have beneficiaries who need one, and have an advocate draft it.
