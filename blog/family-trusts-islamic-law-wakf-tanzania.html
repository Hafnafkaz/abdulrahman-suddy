Family Trusts, Islamic Inheritance and Wakf in Tanzania

For Muslim families, estate planning is a question of faith as well as law. A trust can work with Islamic rules, not against them, if it is designed carefully.

Abdulrahman SuddyBusiness & Financial Advisory · Last updated: 19 November 2026 · 8 min read
Quick answerIn Tanzania, Islamic law generally governs the estates of Muslims, which fixes shares for certain heirs and limits how much can be left by will outside those shares, a limit courts have applied. A wakf is an Islamic endowment for religious, charitable or family purposes and has its own rules under the Probate and Administration of Estates Act. Islamic succession in Tanzania

Courts apply Islamic law to the estates of Muslims, and have looked at whether the deceased actually lived as a practising Muslim. Islamic rules give fixed shares to specified heirs and limit bequests to non-heirs, a limit Tanzanian courts have applied, including to a wakf where heirs existed.

What is a wakf?

A wakf is an endowment dedicated under Islamic law for religious, charitable or benevolent purposes, including family purposes in some forms. The Probate and Administration of Estates Act contains provisions on wakf, and Zanzibar has its own wakf institutions.

Where a trust can help Muslim families

  • Management, not redistribution. Trustees can manage a business or property while heirs' shares are respected.
  • Lifetime gifts. Assets given during life follow different rules from bequests on death. Take advice on how gifts interact with Islamic principles.
  • Charitable giving. A Registered Trustees body or wakf can hold assets for a mosque, madrasa or community project.
  • Protecting minors. Trustees can manage a minor heir's share until they are mature.

Frequently asked questions

Can Muslims create a family trust in Tanzania?

Yes, but it should be designed to respect Islamic succession rules that apply to Muslims' estates. Take advice from an advocate who knows both Tanzanian and Islamic law.

What is the difference between a wakf and a trust?

A wakf is an Islamic endowment with its own religious rules and legal provisions. A trust is a general legal arrangement governed by the trust deed and trust law.

Can a Muslim leave more than the permitted share by will?

Islamic law limits bequests to non-heirs, and Tanzanian courts have applied that limit. Lifetime planning follows different rules, so take specific advice.
