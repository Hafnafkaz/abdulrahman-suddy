How to Register a Trust in Tanzania with RITA: Step-by-Step Guide

Registration turns a group of trustees into a body that can own property, sign documents and sue in its own name. Here is how the process works, from name to certificate.

Abdulrahman SuddyBusiness & Financial Advisory · Last updated: 19 November 2026 · 8 min read
Quick answerTo register a trust in Tanzania, the trustees apply to the Administrator-General through RITA under the Trustees' Incorporation Act (Cap 318). You prepare a trust deed or constitution, choose a name ending in "Registered Trustees", appoint eligible trustees, complete Form TI.1 with supporting documents and recommendation letters, pay the government fee and answer any RITA queries. If approved, you receive a Certificate of Incorporation and the trustees become a body corporate with perpetual succession.

Why register a trust at all?

A trust can exist without registration, as long as there is a clear trust deed and property has been transferred to trustees. Registration (called incorporation) adds something important: the trustees become a single legal body with perpetual succession. That body can own land, open accounts and sign agreements in its own name, and it does not need to re-register every time a trustee changes. Banks, land offices and counterparties are far more comfortable dealing with a registered trust.

Who can register under the Act?

The Trustees' Incorporation Act covers trustees of bodies bound together by custom, religion, kinship or nationality, and trustees holding property for religious, educational, literary, scientific, social or charitable purposes. Family trusts are commonly incorporated on the basis of kinship. Some organisations are expressly excluded and must register under their own laws, including companies, NGOs, trade unions, political parties, sports associations, cooperative societies and VICOBA groups.

The registration steps

  1. Confirm the structure fits the Act. If your aim is profit-making trade, a company may be the right vehicle instead.
  2. Choose a name. It must include the words "Registered Trustees". The Administrator-General can refuse a name considered undesirable.
  3. Appoint eligible trustees. Residency rules apply and people with certain convictions or bankruptcy cannot serve. See choosing trustees.
  4. Prepare the trust deed or constitution. This is the rulebook. See what a trust deed must include.
  5. Hold a founding meeting. Record minutes adopting the deed and appointing trustees.
  6. Complete Form TI.1 and the supporting annexure.
  7. Collect recommendation letters. Published practice lists letters from local government levels such as the Ward Executive Officer and the District Commissioner, plus a sector or religious body recommendation where relevant.
  8. Submit through RITA, including the eRITA online portal, and pay the government fee.
  9. Respond to queries. The Administrator-General may ask for more information or sworn declarations.
  10. Receive the Certificate of Incorporation. Notice of incorporation is published in the Government Gazette.

Documents to prepare

  • Form TI.1 and annexure
  • Trust deed or constitution, usually in duplicate
  • Minutes adopting the deed and appointing trustees
  • Trustee details: identification, passport photographs, CVs and addresses
  • Recommendation letters required for your type of trust
  • Beneficial ownership details for the people who ultimately control or benefit

Approval is not automatic

The Administrator-General has discretion. Meeting the checklist does not guarantee a certificate, and the certificate can carry conditions, for example limits on how much land the trust may hold or how it may be used. Treat the application as a serious legal filing, not a form.

What happens after registration?

Registration is the start, not the finish. Registered trusts must file annual returns, notify changes to trustees or the deed within set time limits, keep proper accounts, and in practice obtain the Administrator-General's consent before acquiring land. Frequently asked questions

Where do I register a trust in Tanzania?

With the Administrator-General through RITA (the Registration, Insolvency and Trusteeship Agency), including its eRITA online portal.

Can a family trust be registered in Tanzania?

Yes. The Trustees' Incorporation Act covers bodies bound by kinship as well as charitable and similar purposes, and family trusts are commonly incorporated on that basis. The Administrator-General decides each application.

What must the name of a registered trust include?

The words "Registered Trustees".

Is registration compulsory for a family trust?

A private trust can exist under a valid deed without incorporation, but registration gives the trustees a single legal identity with perpetual succession, which makes owning and dealing with property much easier. Trustees holding property for charitable or similar purposes are required to apply.
