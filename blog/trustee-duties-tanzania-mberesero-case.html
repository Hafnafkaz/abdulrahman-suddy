Trustee Duties in Tanzania: Lessons from the Mberesero Case

A family set up a foundation, relatives managed its properties, and years later the foundation sued for accounts. The court said no. Here is why, and what every trustee should take from it.

Abdulrahman SuddyBusiness & Financial Advisory · Last updated: 19 November 2026 · 8 min read
Quick answerTrustees in Tanzania owe fiduciary duties: they must act in the beneficiaries' best interests, follow the trust deed, preserve trust property, keep proper records and avoid conflicts. They can be personally liable for breach, mismanagement or negligence. In The Registered Trustees of Alli Mberesero Foundation v Wilfred and George Mberesero (High Court, judgment delivered November 2024), the court held that duties to account and remit profits had not been legally established, because they were never clearly set out. Write duties into the deed.

What happened in the Mberesero case?

As summarised by Victory Attorneys, the registered trustees of a family foundation sued two family members who had been managing properties connected to the foundation. The foundation said they held the properties under a management trust and had failed to render accounts and remit profits.

The High Court (Simfukwe, J.) found that those obligations had not been legally established. There was no lease or contract requiring the defendants to account. Minutes of a family meeting did not say the properties were entrusted on terms requiring accounts. Parts of an agreement relied on were too uncertain to create enforceable duties, applying the rule in the Law of Contract Act that agreements whose meaning is uncertain are void.

The core lesson

Incorporation gives a trust legal personality. It does not tell anyone how trust property must be administered. That is the job of the trust instrument. Family understandings, however sincere, are not a substitute.

The duties trustees owe

  • Loyalty. Act only for the beneficiaries, never for personal gain.
  • Obedience to the deed. Follow its terms and the law.
  • Care. Manage property with the diligence of a prudent person.
  • Preservation. Protect and insure assets, and do not let them waste away.
  • Accounting. Keep proper books and be able to show what came in and went out.
  • Impartiality. Treat beneficiaries fairly according to the deed.
  • No conflicts. Disclose and manage any personal interest.
  • Compliance. Meet registration, tax and reporting obligations.

When are trustees personally liable?

Tanzanian legal commentary notes that trustees can be personally liable for breach of duty, mismanagement or negligence, and accountable for property under their control. A trustee who pays for a breach that was not their fault may seek contribution from co-trustees. That is a strong reason for trustees to keep records and take advice.

A trustee's practical checklist

  1. Read the deed in full and keep a copy.
  2. Make an inventory of trust property and check how each item is registered.
  3. Open a separate bank account in the trust's name.
  4. Minute every decision.
  5. Prepare annual accounts and share them as the deed requires.
  6. Diarise registration filings and tax deadlines.
  7. Get professional advice before major transactions.

Frequently asked questions

What was decided in the Mberesero case?

The High Court found that the duties the foundation relied on, to account for properties and remit profits, had not been legally established, because no clear instrument created them. Family meeting minutes and an uncertain agreement were not enough.

Can a trustee be personally liable in Tanzania?

Yes. Trustees can be personally liable for breach of trust, mismanagement or negligence, and are accountable for the trust property under their control.

Does registering a trust define the trustees' duties?

No. Registration gives the trust legal personality. The trustees' duties come from the trust deed and the general law, so the deed must set them out clearly.
