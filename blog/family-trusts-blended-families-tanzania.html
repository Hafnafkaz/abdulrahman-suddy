Family Trusts for Blended Families and Multiple Marriages in Tanzania

Children from more than one relationship are one of the most common triggers for inheritance disputes. Silence makes it worse. Structure makes it better.

Abdulrahman SuddyBusiness & Financial Advisory · Last updated: 19 November 2026 · 7 min read
Quick answerIn blended families, a family trust can provide for a current spouse during their lifetime while protecting assets for children from earlier relationships, set out fair shares in writing, and separate the property of each marriage. Courts in Tanzania have set aside a will partly because it excluded children from a first marriage without explanation, so clear, early and explained planning matters.

Why blended families face more disputes

  • Different branches may have different expectations.
  • Property from an earlier marriage can get mixed with later assets.
  • A surviving spouse and children from a previous marriage may have competing needs.

In the widely reported Mengi will case, the High Court considered, among other things, that the will excluded children from the first marriage without explanation and included property linked to the first wife.

How a trust helps

  1. Life interest for a spouse. The spouse receives income or a home for life; capital passes to children later.
  2. Separate pots. Assets from each marriage can be kept and recorded separately.
  3. Written reasons. A letter of wishes explains your choices, reducing claims that someone was forgotten.
  4. Neutral trustees. An independent trustee can balance competing interests.

Practical steps

  • List assets and record which marriage or period each came from.
  • Decide how each branch is provided for.
  • Talk to the family while you can explain.
  • Have an advocate draft the trust and will together.

Frequently asked questions

How do I provide for my spouse and children from a previous marriage?

A trust can give your spouse income or a home for life while preserving capital for all your children afterwards, with the terms set out in the deed.

Can excluding children make a will invalid?

In one widely reported Tanzanian case, the High Court set aside a will in part because it excluded children from a first marriage without explanation. Get advice and explain your reasons.

Should I tell my family about my trust?

Generally yes. Explaining your plan while alive is one of the most effective ways to prevent disputes.
