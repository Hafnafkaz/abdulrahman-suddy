Family Trust vs Holding Company in Tanzania: Differences and How to Combine Them

A holding company organises who owns the businesses. A trust decides who benefits and who looks after it. Confusing the two leads to expensive structures that do not do what the family expected.

Abdulrahman SuddyBusiness & Financial Advisory · Last updated: 19 November 2026 · 7 min read
Quick answerA holding company is a company that owns shares in other companies, useful for separating risk and organising several ventures. A family trust is an arrangement where trustees hold assets for beneficiaries under a trust deed, useful for succession, protecting dependants and keeping ownership together. A common structure in Tanzania is a holding company that owns the operating businesses, with a family trust owning the holding company's shares, plus a will for everything else.

The combined structure

  1. Operating companies own their own assets and trade.
  2. A holding company owns the operating companies.
  3. A family trust owns the holding company's shares.
  4. Trustees vote the shares according to the deed; directors run the business.
  5. A will covers anything outside the structure.

The result: one clear block of ownership, management separated from inheritance, and continuity if the founder dies.

Lessons from Tanzanian courts

In the Rwakatare estate case, the Court of Appeal confirmed that heirs inherit a deceased shareholder's shares, not the company's assets. Registration details decide ownership. See the Rwakatare case.

Costs and caution

Moving shares into a holding company or a trust is a transfer that can carry stamp duty and tax consequences. Model it first with a tax adviser. Read holding companies in Tanzania and how trusts are taxed.

Frequently asked questions

Should I use a trust or a holding company?

Use a holding company to organise ownership of businesses and separate risk. Use a trust to decide who benefits and to keep ownership together across generations. Many families use both.

Can a trust own a company in Tanzania?

Yes. Trustees can hold shares, and a registered trust can hold them in its own name. The shares must be properly transferred and recorded in the company's register.

Does a holding company replace a will?

No. Holding company shares still pass through your estate unless they are held in a trust.
